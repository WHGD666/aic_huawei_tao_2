DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE (mechanisms, not recipes):
- Setting: a flat netlist is split into two tiers (bottom / upper) of a face-to-face stacked 3D IC. Every net whose pins span both tiers becomes a cross-tier net routed through a hybrid-bonding terminal (HBT): a fixed passive element that adds delay and load. After partitioning, a FIXED flow runs: 2-tier analytical placement -> legalisation -> power network -> HBT assignment -> one timing-repair pass -> STA. The score is read after STA. The partition can only influence timing through: (a) how many and WHICH nets are cut (critical paths crossing tiers pay HBT delay every time), (b) how the balance-repair step mangles the learned structure, (c) how critical logic and its area are distributed between tiers (per-tier congestion and buffer room).
- Inputs available to the objective: hypergraph (nets -> pins), per-node area and macro flag, and per-node 2D timing features from a 2D implementation of the same circuit: slack, slew, arrival, required, cell_delay, timing_coverage, plus degree and max fan-out. The baseline objectives ignore the timing features entirely. 2D criticality is a useful but imperfect predictor of 3D criticality: use it as a soft weight (quantile-based), not as a hard rule.
- Balance constraints checked after the official repair step: upper-tier cell area 47-53% of total; when macros exist, upper-tier macro COUNT ratio and macro AREA ratio both 45-55%. The repair step flips nodes greedily until constraints hold. If the raw partition is far from balanced, repair flips many nodes and destroys whatever the objective learned ("repair flips" statistic). An objective that lands near 50% by itself keeps its structure.
- Expected-cut formula 1 - prod(p) - prod(1-p) saturates at ~1 for nets with many pins (clock / reset / high fan-out): gradient vanishes on exactly the nets that span the design. Alternatives: pairwise / star expansion (sum over pins of (p_i - mean_p_net)^2), soft-range via logsumexp, or fan-out-based down-weighting (1/log(k)).
- Quadratic balance penalties are flat near 50%; a hinge outside a small dead-band (|ratio-0.5| > 0.01) with a large coefficient keeps the raw partition inside the band without fighting the cut term. Macro balance (count and area) needs its own term when macros exist; guard the no-macro case.
- Critical-path locality: adjacent critical nodes (both with low slack quantile) should share a tier; a Laplacian-style term c_i*c_j*(p_i-p_j)^2 on edges, or criticality-weighted net-cut weights (1 + alpha*max_pin_crit), both express this. Keep alpha moderate: over-weighting critical nets makes everything else get cut.
- Timing-repair asymmetry is possible in the fixed flow (e.g. buffers only allowed on one tier). If so, placing critical logic on the repairable tier helps; this is a hypothesis to probe, not a fact.
- For the representation-learning baseline: positive pairs can be weighted by net criticality or normalised by net fan-out (clique weight 1/(k-1)) so hub nets do not collapse all nodes; the decoder is a deterministic algorithm where a criticality-weighted FM/KL refinement under the balance constraint is usually worth more than any change to the embedding loss.
- Validity killers (candidate becomes INVALID and is worthless): NaN/Inf loss, zero gradient, a loss that does not depend on the prediction, repair failure, exceptions on edge cases (no macros, isolated nodes, empty nets), runtime blow-up (Python loops over nodes), non-deterministic decode.
- Budget: 30 evaluations total; each is 9 full flows. One attributable mechanism per candidate; prefer TNS (60% weight, tie-breaker) over chasing a single worst path; prefer changes that generalise over tuning.
